The PCDID Approach to Treatment Effects Estimation: A Further Investigation

Tilman Bretschneider & Joakim Westerlund — Journal of Applied Econometrics, 41(2), 2026

In the present paper, we study the so-called “PCDID” approach to treatment effects estimation in panels with interactive effects where the factors represent trends whose effect need not be parallel across the cross-sectional units. Our interest in this step-wise approach originates with the observation that the interactive effects are ignored in the first step, which should not be possible without affecting subsequent steps. We confirm that the estimated second-step factors are inconsistent in the empirically relevant scenario where the included covariates are correlated with the true factors. Interestingly enough, though, fixed effects demeaning, which is in theory unnecessary since fixed effects are special interactive effects, is shown to restore consistency of the final-step average treatment effects estimate; however, only under special circumstances. As a general solution to the inconsistency of the estimated factors, we propose an iterated PCDID version.

Factor-Based Imputation of Missing Values Using Cross-Sectional Averages

Tilman Bretschneider & Yousef Kaddoura

Cross-sectional averages are widely used to impute missing values in panel data, and are separately known to proxy the latent factors driving a panel. Combining these two observations, we propose a new factor-based imputation method. We use cross-sectional averages as factor estimates, recover unit-specific loadings by least squares, and impute missing entries with the fitted common component. We derive the asymptotic properties of the estimated factors, loadings, and imputations under large N and large T asymptotics, accommodating general missing-data patterns. We further allow the number of factors to be overspecified, a case most existing approaches rule out, and show that overspecification leaves consistency unaffected but alters the limiting distribution. For inference, we establish asymptotic results of variance estimators when a block of units is fully observed, and propose two bootstrap procedures for general missing patterns, one for confidence intervals and another for prediction intervals. Applied to FRED-SD, a U.S. state-level macroeconomic panel, the method clearly outperforms cross-sectional average and median imputation and compares favourably to principal-component-based approaches. We further use it to construct monthly state-level GDP growth indices that track quarterly GDP closely.
